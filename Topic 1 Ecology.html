🌍 TOPIC 1: Introduction to Ecology and Environmental Health

Ecological Principles, Levels of Organization & Environmental Health Practice

1. What is Ecology?

Let's begin with a simple question.

Imagine a mosquito living near a stagnant pond:

  • It needs water to reproduce.
  • It needs a suitable temperature to survive.
  • It interacts with humans and other animals.
  • Humans may create or remove its breeding sites.

Can you see something interesting? The mosquito does not live independently. Its life depends on its surroundings and interactions with other organisms. That is the central idea of ecology!

Definition

Ecology is the scientific study of the interactions between living organisms and their environment.

The word ecology comes from two Greek words:

WordMeaning
OikosHouse or home
LogosStudy

Therefore, ecology literally means the study of our home or surroundings.

Concept of ecosystem: Organisms interacting with their environment

Understand the Definition in Two Parts

Ecology studies:

  • Organisms: Humans, animals, plants, bacteria and other living things.
  • Environment: Everything surrounding those organisms that affects their lives.
🌾 For example, consider a farmer:
  • The farmer is the organism.
  • Soil, rainfall, temperature, sunlight and other organisms are parts of the environment.
  • Their interactions determine how successfully crops grow.
📌 Important: Ecology does not simply study organisms. It studies the relationships between organisms and their surroundings.

2. What is the Environment?

Think of the environment as the entire neighborhood in which an organism lives. It includes living and non-living things.

We classify environmental components into two major groups:

A. Biotic Factors (Living Components)

Biotic means living. Examples:

  • Humans
  • Animals
  • Plants
  • Bacteria
  • Fungi
  • Parasites

B. Abiotic Factors (Non-Living Components)

Abiotic means non-living physical and chemical components. Examples:

  • Water
  • Air
  • Temperature
  • Sunlight
  • Soil
  • Humidity
  • Minerals
Ecosystem diagram: Biotic and abiotic components

Let's Apply It to Malaria

Imagine a community where malaria is common:

ComponentExample
BioticHumans, mosquitoes and Plasmodium parasites
AbioticTemperature, rainfall and stagnant water

Why does this matter? Because malaria transmission depends on interactions between living organisms and environmental conditions. For example, rainfall may create mosquito breeding sites, increasing the opportunity for transmission.

⚠️ Exam trick: Mosquito = biotic. Stagnant water = abiotic. Do not confuse them!

3. Levels of Ecological Organization

This is one of the most important classification sections. Imagine starting with one person and gradually expanding your view until you see the entire planet. Ecology organizes living systems into different levels.

Hierarchy: Organism up to the global Biosphere

Let's understand each level using humans and other organisms:

Level 1: Organism

A single living individual.
Example: One human, one mosquito or one bacterium.

Level 2: Species

A group of organisms similar to one another, belonging to the same biological species.
Example: Humans (Homo sapiens).

Level 3: Population

A group of individuals of the same species living in the same area.
Example: All mosquitoes of one species living around a particular pond.
Key: Same species + same area = population.

Level 4: Community

Different populations living together in a defined area.
Example: Mosquitoes, frogs, fish, algae and bacteria living around a pond.
Key: Different populations living together = community.

Level 5: Ecosystem

A collection of organisms interacting with one another and with the non-living environment.
Example: A pond containing fish, frogs, algae, water, sunlight, minerals and soil.

🔍 Notice the difference:
• Community = living populations.
• Ecosystem = living populations + non-living environment + interactions.

Level 6: Biome

A group of ecosystems with similar climates and similar dominant biological communities.
Examples: Tropical rainforest, desert and grassland.

Level 7: Biosphere

The broadest level: the global zone of Earth where life exists. It includes all Earth's ecosystems.

Remember the Order from Smallest to Largest

LevelSummary Description
OrganismOne living individual
SpeciesSimilar organisms belonging to a species
PopulationSame species in one area
CommunityDifferent populations living together
EcosystemLiving + non-living components + interactions
BiomeSimilar ecosystems and climate
BiosphereAll regions of Earth where life exists

4. Branches of Ecology

Think of ecology as a big subject with different departments. Each department studies a different level of interaction.

A. Population Ecology

Studies how populations of the same species grow, decrease and reproduce in relation to environmental factors.

Example: Why does the mosquito population increase during the rainy season? Scientists may investigate:

  • Food availability
  • Breeding sites
  • Predators
  • Competition
  • Environmental conditions

B. Community Ecology

Studies interactions between different populations within an environment.
Example: How do mosquitoes, frogs, fish and plants interact in a pond?

C. Landscape Ecology

Studies relationships between ecological processes and the landscape, including landscape diversity.
Example: How does deforestation in one area affect surrounding ecosystems?

D. Ecosystem Ecology

Studies the integrated interactions between living and non-living components of an ecosystem.
Example: How do sunlight, soil, water, plants and animals work together in a forest?

Branch Comparison

BranchMain Focus
Population ecologyOne species' population
Community ecologyDifferent populations
Landscape ecologyEcological processes across landscapes
Ecosystem ecologyLiving and non-living interactions
⚠️ Exam trick: If a question mentions the interaction of fish and frogs, think community ecology. If it includes water, sunlight and soil too, think ecosystem ecology.

5. What is Environmental Health?

Now let's connect ecology to medicine and public health.

Imagine a village where:

  • People drink contaminated water.
  • Waste is dumped near houses.
  • Mosquitoes breed in stagnant water.
  • Houses have poor ventilation.

People living there may develop diarrhea, malaria and respiratory diseases. A doctor can treat sick individuals, but can treatment alone solve all these problems? No! We must also improve the environment. This is where environmental health becomes important.

Definition

Environmental health comprises the physical, chemical, biological, social and psychosocial aspects of the environment that affect human health and survival.

It also refers to the theory and practice of:

  • Assessing environmental factors.
  • Correcting harmful factors.
  • Controlling environmental hazards.
  • Preventing environmental factors that may affect the health of present and future generations.

Understand the Five Environmental Aspects

AspectExample
PhysicalTemperature, noise, radiation
ChemicalPesticides, toxic gases, chemicals
BiologicalBacteria, viruses, parasites
SocialHousing conditions, community relationships
PsychosocialStress, fear, social pressure
🏭 Let's apply them to a practical scenario:
A factory releases toxic chemicals near a residential area:
  • Chemical: Toxic substances.
  • Physical: Noise from machinery.
  • Biological: Possible microbial contamination of nearby water.
  • Social: Displacement or disruption of the community.
  • Psychosocial: Stress and anxiety among residents.
One environment can affect health through several pathways simultaneously.

6. Scope of Environmental Health

Scope means the areas covered by a subject. Your lecture identifies these major areas:

1. Safe Water Supply

Providing adequate, accessible and safe water to communities.

Safe water testing and municipal distribution

2. Waste Management

Proper management of solid and liquid waste.

Proper solid and liquid waste management

3. Vector Control

Controlling insects, rodents and other disease vectors.

Mosquito and disease vector abatement

4. Food Hygiene

Preventing food contamination and foodborne illness.

Food safety practices and contamination prevention

5. Residential and Institutional Health

Maintaining healthy homes, schools, hospitals and other institutions.

Healthy housing conditions and proper ventilation

6. Occupational Health and Safety

Protecting workers against workplace hazards.

Occupational health protection in the workplace

7. Prevention of Biosphere Pollution

Preventing pollution of air, water and soil.

Air, water, and soil pollution abatement
📌 Important Distinction:
• Ecology studies relationships between organisms and their environment.
• Environmental health focuses on assessing and managing environmental factors that affect human health.
They are closely related, but they are not exactly the same subject.

7. Clinical and Public-Health Application

Let's test whether you can connect the concepts.

A community experiences frequent diarrhea outbreaks.

Investigation reveals:

  • Drinking water comes from a contaminated river.
  • Human waste is disposed of near the river.
  • People do not consistently wash their hands.

An environmental health professional may:

  • Assess the water source.
  • Identify contamination pathways.
  • Improve sanitation.
  • Promote hand hygiene.
  • Ensure safe drinking water.
💡 Notice something important: Instead of treating only the patients, we also address the environmental causes of disease. That's the practical importance of environmental health.
