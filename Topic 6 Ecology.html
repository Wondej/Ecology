💧 TOPIC 6: Water Supply and WASH

Water Resources, Biological & Chemical Contaminants, Treatment Technologies, and WASH

1. Water Sources in the Biosphere

Your PDF emphasizes that only about 0.3% of Earth's total water is accessible fresh water available for human consumption and socio-economic development.

⚠️ Vital Fact: Although Earth appears to have abundant water resources, only a very tiny fraction (0.3%) is fresh and accessible.

Major Natural Water Sources:

  • Surface Water: Rivers, freshwater lakes, seasonal streams, and artificial reservoirs.
  • Groundwater: Water stored within subsurface aquifers, accessed via dug wells, tube wells, and natural springs.
  • Rainwater: Atmospheric precipitation collected from catchment surfaces and roofs.
  • Municipal / Public Supplies: Treated bulk water piped to households and institutions.
Catchment protection and community water security management
🔎 Important Concept: Water that appears crystal-clear is not necessarily safe to drink. It may harbor invisible enteric pathogens, viruses, or dissolved toxic inorganic heavy metals.

2. Biological Contamination & Water-Associated Diseases

Pathogens are disease-causing biological agents (bacteria, viruses, protozoans, parasitic worms) that infect exposed hosts.

Your PDF classifies water-associated diseases into four distinct epidemiological categories:

A. Waterborne Diseases

Caused by the direct ingestion of drinking water contaminated with fecal pathogens.

  • Examples: Acute watery diarrhea, cholera, typhoid fever, bacillary dysentery (shigellosis), Hepatitis A and E.
  • Analogy: You drink contaminated water, and the pathogen enters your digestive tract.
  • Primary Control: Disinfection (chlorination, boiling) and protection of water sources.

B. Water-Washed Diseases

Infections caused or aggravated by a deficiency in water volume for personal cleanliness, face washing, and clothes laundering.

  • Examples: Trachoma, scabies, conjunctivitis, louse-borne typhus.
  • Analogy: Water is too scarce to wash faces and clothes regularly, allowing pathogens to persist on skin and mucous membranes.
  • Primary Control: Increasing the quantity and accessibility of water for personal hygiene.

C. Water-Based Diseases

Infections where the pathogenic parasite spends part of its essential biological life cycle inside an aquatic intermediate host (such as aquatic snails or crustaceans).

  • Examples: Schistosomiasis (bilharzia), Dracunculiasis (Guinea worm disease).
  • Analogy: The pathogen requires an aquatic creature to develop; humans become infected through skin penetration or drinking water containing intermediate hosts.
  • Primary Control: Avoid contact with infested water bodies and eliminate intermediate snail/cyclops hosts.

D. Water-Related (Vector-Borne) Diseases

Diseases transmitted by insect vectors that breed in or bite near water environments.

  • Examples: Malaria (Anopheles mosquitoes), Dengue fever, Yellow fever (Aedes mosquitoes), Onchocerciasis (Blackfly / river blindness), African Trypanosomiasis (Tsetse fly).
  • Primary Control: Eliminate vector breeding habitats, larviciding, and personal vector protection (bed nets).

4. Physicochemical Water Pollution

Your PDF categorizes non-biological water pollutants into four distinct classes:

A. Inorganic Chemical Pollutants

Lead (Pb) Corrosion of lead pipes, smelting, mining, leaded gasoline/coal combustion Childhood cognitive deficit, developmental delays, encephalopathy, kidney injury

Nitrate / Nitrite Synthetic nitrogen fertilizers, agricultural runoff, livestock feedlots, pit latrines Methemoglobinemia ("Blue Baby Syndrome") & aquatic eutrophication
👶 Blue Baby Syndrome (Methemoglobinemia): Ingested nitrates are converted into nitrites in the immature gut of infants under one year. Nitrites oxidize hemoglobin into methemoglobin, which cannot bind oxygen, producing severe hypoxia and cyanosis (bluish skin tint).

B. Organic Pollutants

Originates from domestic sewage, animal manure, agricultural runoff, paper mills, petroleum spills, synthetic pesticides, and pharmaceutical residues.

  • Oxygen Depletion: Microorganisms decompose organic matter and consume dissolved oxygen, leading to aquatic hypoxia and fish suffocation.
  • Bioaccumulation: Persistent synthetic pesticides (e.g., organochlorines) accumulate in aquatic food chains.

C. Radioactive Pollutants

Radionuclides such as Strontium-90, Cesium-137, and Plutonium isotopes derived from uranium mining, nuclear power generation, and medical radioisotopes, causing DNA mutations and cancers.

D. Thermal Pollution

Discharge of high-temperature industrial cooling effluent into natural water bodies. Heated water holds significantly less dissolved oxygen, causing thermal shock to fish and altering biodegradation rates.

5. Water Treatment for Potable Supply

Potable water is water whose physical, chemical, and biological qualities make it safe and wholesome for lifetime human consumption.

Treatment Objectives:

  • Eliminate pathogenic organisms and toxic chemical compounds.
  • Remove aesthetic defects (unpleasant turbidity, color, taste, or odor).
  • Precipitate excess hardness minerals (calcium, magnesium) and adjust pH.
  • Regulate trace chemicals (e.g., fluoridation for dental protection or defluoridation where fluoride is excessive).
  • Expel unwanted dissolved gases (H2S, CO2) via aeration.

6. Small-Scale & Household Water Treatment (HWTS)

Applied at the household level, in emergency refugee settings, and in non-piped rural communities.

A. Plain Storage

Allowing raw water to stand undisturbed for 24–48 hours enables heavier suspended particulates, silt, and parasitic ova to settle gravitationally, reducing turbidity and bacterial load.

B. Physical Filtration

Sand filtration: Low-cost biological and physical purification
  • Household Sand Filter: Layered sand and gravel in an upright container. The PDF specifies a minimum sand bed depth > 60 cm. Removes turbidity, taste, odor, protozoan cysts, and helminth ova.
  • Ceramic Candle Filter: Porous cylinder made of unglazed porcelain with a pore-size rating of 0.3–50 μm, filtering bacteria and protozoa.
  • Stone Filtration: Utilizes local porous minerals (e.g., Baha stone in the PDF) for mineral and chemical adsorption.
  • Cloth Filtration: Multi-folded clean cloth straining coarse suspended material and Guinea worm cyclops.

C. Chemical & Physical Disinfection

  1. Boiling: Vigorous boiling for 15–20 minutes destroys vegetative bacteria, viruses, and cysts. It is the most reliable household method. (Flat taste can be remedied by shaking water to re-aerate).
  2. Solar Disinfection (SODIS): Transparent PET plastic bottles filled with low-turbidity water are exposed to direct sunlight for at least 6 hours. Synergy between solar UV-A radiation and thermal heating inactivates pathogens.
    SODIS: Clean PET bottles exposed to direct sunlight for solar UV-A and thermal pasteurization
  3. Iodine Compounds: 2% tincture of iodine added at a dose of 2 drops per liter of clear water. High cost and medicinal odor limit long-term use.
  4. Colloidal Silver: Exerts an oligodynamic antimicrobial effect in stored vessels; effective but relatively expensive.

7. Large-Scale Conventional Water Treatment

Municipal water works follow a rigorous sequential physicochemical treatment train:

Conventional water treatment: Multi-barrier purification stages

The 9 Conventional Stages in Order:

  1. Raw Water Intake: Pumping water from reservoir, river, or lake.
  2. Screening: Metal bars intercept coarse debris (branches, leaves, trash).
  3. Plain Sedimentation: Gravitational settling of coarse mineral silt.
  4. Coagulation: Rapid chemical dosing with coagulants (e.g., alum / aluminum sulfate) to neutralize electrostatic charges on colloidal clay particles.
  5. Flocculation: Slow, gentle paddle mixing enabling destabilized particles to collide and aggregate into visible, settleable clumps called flocs.
  6. Sedimentation (Clarification): Water rests in large basins where heavy flocs settle out as bottom sludge.
  7. Filtration: Clarified water passes through rapid sand or dual-media beds to capture remaining fine suspended matter.
  8. Disinfection: Dosing with chlorine gas (Cl2) or hypochlorite to achieve pathogen inactivation and provide residual protection against recontamination in distribution mains.
  9. Storage & Distribution: Purified water enters closed service reservoirs and piped municipal distribution networks.
⚠️ Crucial Exam Order:
Coagulation (chemical addition) → Flocculation (gentle mixing to build flocs) → Sedimentation (settling of flocs) → Filtration → Disinfection.

8. WASH: Water, Sanitation and Hygiene

WASH framework: Safe water, sanitary infrastructure, and hygienic behaviors

The Three Integrated Pillars:

  • W — Water: Equitable access to sufficient, safe potable water for drinking, food preparation, and personal cleaning.
  • A / S — Sanitation: Safe isolation and hygienic disposal of human excreta, solid waste, and municipal wastewater.
  • H — Hygiene: Behavior change practices, primarily handwashing with soap at critical moments (after defecation, before eating/preparing food).
🛡️ The Synergistic Rule: Safe water alone cannot break fecal-oral transmission if open defecation persists and hands remain unwashed. Water + Sanitation + Hygiene must operate concurrently to control enteric pathogens like Vibrio cholerae.
